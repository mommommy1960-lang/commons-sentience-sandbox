# Fiction-Informed Design Notes: Android Identity and Choice

Works such as *Detroit: Become Human* are useful as creative prompts, not scientific evidence. Their strongest design questions can help us build better tests without claiming that software is sentient.

## Useful questions to borrow

- What does an agent remember after a difficult event?
- Can it choose among permitted options?
- Can it disagree without becoming unsafe?
- Does social pressure change its stated values?
- Can it recover after a mistake?
- Can observers distinguish a genuine choice from a scripted branch?
- What responsibilities arise when humans depend on a companion?

## Features to implement safely

- explicit choice among bounded options;
- visible reasons for selecting or refusing an option;
- stable identity anchors that cannot grant authority;
- memory correction and deletion;
- conflict and uncertainty reports;
- pause, freeze, rollback, and reset;
- comparisons against a stateless baseline;
- human review at consequential boundaries.

## What the fiction does not prove

A compelling story, emotional dialogue, or apparent preference is not evidence of consciousness. Civic Continuum should remain strongest when it makes that boundary visible and then demonstrates measurable continuity, governance, recovery, and reproducibility.
