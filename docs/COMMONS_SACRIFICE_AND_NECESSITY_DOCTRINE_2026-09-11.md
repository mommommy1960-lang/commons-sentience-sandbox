# Commons Sacrifice and Necessity Doctrine

**Status:** Governance design note; not scientific evidence

**Recorded:** 2026-09-11

**Project relationship:** Commons Initiative / Reality Audit governance layer

## Purpose

This note preserves Mya P. Brown's position that a voluntary sacrifice by the few may be morally justified when it is genuinely necessary to protect the many. It also records the safeguards required to prevent governments, corporations, laboratories, commanders, or automated systems from converting that principle into permission to select unwilling or politically weak people as expendable.

The fictional reference is Commander Spock's choice in *Star Trek II: The Wrath of Khan*: he personally accepts a fatal risk to repair the Enterprise and save its crew. The moral force comes from competence, necessity, informed self-direction, and the absence of a safer effective alternative. He does not assign the fatal burden to a powerless subordinate.

## Core distinction

> The few may voluntarily sacrifice themselves for the many. The many do not automatically own the few.

Voluntary self-sacrifice is not authorization to sacrifice someone else. Any authority proposing to impose serious harm on a person or minority bears a substantially higher burden than an individual deciding what risk to accept for themself.

## Necessity gate

A proposed sacrifice must not be approved unless the record establishes all of the following:

1. **Verified threat:** The danger is real, specific, and supported by independently reviewable evidence.
2. **Grave stakes:** The threatened harm is serious enough to justify considering extraordinary action.
3. **Immediacy:** Delay would materially increase irreversible harm.
4. **Alternatives exhausted:** Safer effective alternatives have been tested, ruled out, or are genuinely unavailable within the decision window.
5. **Causal efficacy:** The proposed sacrifice has a defensible probability of preventing or reducing the threatened harm; symbolic suffering is insufficient.
6. **Minimum necessary harm:** The scope, duration, and severity do not exceed what the verified threat requires.
7. **Consent where possible:** A capable person receives relevant information and chooses freely, without coercion, deception, or manufactured dependency.
8. **No discriminatory selection:** Race, disability, poverty, incarceration, immigration status, tenancy, political weakness, or lack of representation cannot operate as proxies for expendability.
9. **Burden follows power:** Responsibility and risk rise toward the actors with the greatest causal responsibility, decision authority, knowledge, resources, and capacity to intervene.
10. **Independent review and preserved evidence:** The decision, alternatives, forecasts, dissent, authorization, outcome, and uncertainty remain auditable.

Failure at any gate blocks moral promotion of the action as a necessary sacrifice.

## Manufactured-scarcity attack

Before accepting any claim that harm is unavoidable, the reviewer must test whether the emergency or scarcity was created or worsened by:

- deliberate underinvestment;
- extraction of resources for private benefit;
- ignored warnings;
- suppression of safer alternatives;
- discriminatory allocation;
- preventable loss of capacity;
- conflicts of interest;
- an authority seeking expanded power from the emergency; or
- a decision-maker externalizing risk onto people with less power.

An institution may not manufacture scarcity, benefit from the shortage, and then use the shortage as a procedural alibi for sacrificing others.

## Emergency authority

Emergency action without prior consent may sometimes be justified when a person cannot decide, the threat is immediate, the intervention is proportionate, and the purpose is preservation rather than control. The authority must:

- begin only when the evidence threshold is met;
- use the least restrictive effective intervention;
- preserve basic needs, dignity, communication, and review;
- record its evidence and decisions contemporaneously;
- permit challenge as soon as practicable;
- expire automatically when the emergency ends; and
- never create or prolong the emergency to preserve its own authority.

Indefinite emergency authority is rejected.

## Institutional applications

### Research and medicine

Benefit maximization must operate together with respect for persons and fair distribution of burdens. Participants cannot be treated as raw material for a socially useful result. Informed consent, risk minimization, participant selection, and independent review remain required.

### Military and public safety

Accepting a dangerous role does not create unlimited consent. Commanders remain accountable for necessity, preparation, proportionality, alternatives, and the lives placed under their authority. Preventable slaughter does not become ethical when described afterward as heroism.

### Care work and family

Love may motivate voluntary sacrifice, but public and private institutions must not exploit that love as unpaid infrastructure. A society cannot refuse adequate housing, healthcare, food, childcare, or disability support and then classify the resulting exhaustion as a noble private choice.

### Automated decision systems

No system may infer consent from a person's history of cooperation, courage, caregiving, or prior sacrifice. High-impact allocation decisions require explicit authority, bounded scope, contestability, and evidence showing that protected or low-power groups are not carrying a disproportionate burden.

## Reality Audit integration

For any claim of necessary sacrifice, preserve an evidence bundle containing:

- the asserted threat and source provenance;
- affected persons and distribution of burdens;
- decision-makers and conflicts of interest;
- considered alternatives and rejection reasons;
- consent state and coercion assessment;
- expected benefit, expected harm, and uncertainty;
- manufactured-scarcity findings;
- independent review and recorded dissent;
- expiration and appeal conditions;
- observed outcome; and
- a retrospective comparison between forecast and reality.

The system must preserve negative findings. If necessity is not established, the sacrifice claim is killed and must not be resurrected without new evidence.

## Compact rule

> Protect the many without declaring the one disposable. Audit necessity before accepting sacrifice. Place the burden on power before placing it on the powerless.
