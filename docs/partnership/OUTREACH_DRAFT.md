# Commons Sentience Sandbox — Outreach Draft

## Short description

Commons Sentience Sandbox is a local, rule-governed research platform for comparing two simulated agents with different priorities over long interaction sequences.

Sentinel is continuity-first and governance-strict. Aster is more exploratory and socially adaptive. The sandbox records decisions, memory changes, contradictions, reflections, trust changes, and final state so reviewers can inspect the run instead of relying on a polished conversation.

The project does not claim sentience, consciousness, or general intelligence.

## Why a reviewer may care

The project turns questions about memory, governance, trust, contradiction, and recovery into repeatable experiments. It is designed to run locally, preserve raw artifacts, and expose both successful and failed cases.

Current work includes:

- 30-turn paired-agent challenge sessions;
- a 400-case campaign across families, severities, and seeds;
- reviewer-facing documentation;
- security-hardened CI;
- a roadmap for independent scoring, baselines, memory-risk tests, replay hashes, and outside-lab replication.

## What we are seeking

We welcome:

- university researchers who want to reproduce or challenge the experiments;
- educators who want a hands-on AI-governance teaching example;
- software teams interested in auditable agent workflows;
- reviewers who can identify weaknesses or propose stronger tests.

Participation is not a purchase commitment. The preferred first step is independent review of the methods and artifacts.

## Boundaries

A successful simulation run shows that the code produced a specified behavior under specified conditions. It does not prove that the agents are conscious, generally intelligent, safe in every environment, or better than every other system.
