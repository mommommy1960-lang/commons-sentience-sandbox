# Independent Review and Collaboration Targets

**Prepared:** 2026-09-16  
**Owner:** Mya P. Brown / Commons Initiative

## Ownership boundary

The Commons Initiative remains the owner of its code, documents, trademarks, and inventions. A reviewer or collaborator receives only the access needed for a defined review. No repository transfer, copyright assignment, exclusive license, joint-venture authority, or control over publication is implied.

Use a written review agreement stating:

- background IP remains with the Commons Initiative;
- the reviewer receives a limited, non-exclusive, revocable evaluation license;
- feedback may be incorporated without transferring ownership;
- confidential material is shared only after a separate NDA or equivalent agreement;
- publication requires written approval and correct attribution;
- access ends when the review ends;
- no one may merge, relicense, patent, or commercially use the work without a separate signed agreement.

## Best first request

Ask each new target for one small, bounded action:

> Would you be willing to review one five-minute Sentinel-versus-Aster run and identify the strongest methodological weakness? We are not asking for endorsement, investment, or transfer of ownership. We are seeking criticism that can improve a reproducible, local research platform.

That request is easier to accept and produces more useful evidence than asking someone to “get involved” broadly.

## Suggested review packet

Send only:

1. the five-minute demo guide;
2. the research landscape review;
3. one representative raw result;
4. the independent evaluator;
5. the limitations statement;
6. the ownership boundary above.

Keep unpublished inventions, credentials, private data, and detailed hardware designs out of the first contact.

## What will make the sandbox appealing

The front door should show, in this order:

1. what the sandbox is;
2. what Sentinel and Aster are different about;
3. how to run the five-minute demo;
4. what is measured;
5. one successful trace and one failure trace;
6. how an outside lab reproduces it;
7. exactly what the project does not claim;
8. how to request a bounded review.

The appeal should come from clarity and evidence: a visitor can understand the experiment quickly, inspect the logs, reproduce it without paid services, and challenge the result without asking permission from the owner.
