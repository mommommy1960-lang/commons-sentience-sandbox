# Washington Essential Food Continuity and Surplus Recovery Pilot Act

**MODEL DISCUSSION DRAFT — NOT INTRODUCED, ENACTED, OR REVIEWED BY LEGISLATIVE COUNSEL**

This document supplies policy architecture for qualified Washington legislative counsel. Citations, agency assignments, appropriations, thresholds, liability language, and codification must be verified before introduction.

## Section 1. Short title

This act may be known as the Washington Essential Food Continuity and Surplus Recovery Pilot Act.

## Section 2. Findings and intent

The legislature finds that edible food may be discarded while residents experience food insecurity; that recovery requires safe handling, refrigeration, transportation, staff, and predictable coordination; and that unfunded donation mandates can transfer cost and risk to small businesses and community organizations. The legislature intends to test a supported, transparent, privacy-preserving recovery system before considering statewide permanence or expansion.

## Section 3. Definitions for counsel refinement

- **Covered food business:** a business above a scale threshold set in statute or rule after capacity analysis; very small businesses are exempt unless they opt in.
- **Eligible food:** food that may lawfully and safely be sold, donated, or redistributed under applicable safety standards.
- **Recovery partner:** a public, nonprofit, tribal, cooperative, or other qualified organization with demonstrated handling capacity.
- **Implementing agency:** the state agency designated by final legislation after an authority and capacity review.
- **Independent evaluator:** an entity organizationally separate from delivery and enforcement functions, selected through an open process.

## Section 4. Recovery hierarchy

Covered entities shall use a feasible hierarchy: prevent surplus; sell or discount usable inventory; donate eligible food; direct suitable material to animal feed or lawful industrial use; compost; and dispose only when higher uses are unsafe or infeasible. Nothing in this act requires donation or distribution of unsafe food.

## Section 5. Supported participation

The implementing agency shall establish grants or service contracts for refrigeration, transport, reusable containers, safety training, scheduling, and administrative capacity. Rules shall use phased duties based on organizational capacity. No community recovery partner shall be required to accept food, enter an exclusive arrangement, or absorb uncompensated material costs.

## Section 6. Pilot regions and term

The agency shall select a limited and diverse set of pilot regions through published criteria and community consultation. The operational pilot lasts three years after launch. Expansion outside pilot authority requires subsequent legislative action.

## Section 7. Access and nondiscrimination

Program access may not require disclosure beyond what is necessary to deliver service and evaluate aggregate outcomes. Delivery partners shall provide reasonable disability access and language access and may not discriminate under applicable law. Rules should minimize identification, proof-of-need, and travel burdens.

## Section 8. Data protection

Only data necessary for safety, operations, payment, audit, and evaluation may be collected. Personally identifying recipient data may not be sold, used for advertising, immigration enforcement, unrelated policing, or eligibility decisions outside this program. Public reporting must use aggregation and suppression appropriate to reidentification risk. Retention periods and deletion duties must be published.

## Section 9. Public accountability

The agency shall publish, at least quarterly, aggregate pounds offered, accepted, safely distributed, rejected for safety, and disposed; grant and contract recipients; administrative cost; service coverage; complaints and resolution times; safety incidents; and known data limitations. Reports must distinguish measured facts from estimates.

## Section 10. Complaints, appeal, and correction

Covered entities, recovery partners, workers, and service recipients may submit accessible complaints without retaliation. Material enforcement decisions require written reasons and an appeal to a body independent from the original decision maker. The agency shall publish correction timelines and aggregate dispositions.

## Section 11. Anti-capture and procurement

Contracts and standards shall be awarded through open, reviewable processes. Conflicts of interest and beneficial ownership required by applicable law shall be disclosed. No vendor may receive an exclusive right to the statewide exchange of recovered food or control access to public program data. Interoperable export and transition assistance are required at contract end.

## Section 12. Independent evaluation

Before operations begin, the evaluator shall publish a measurement plan with baselines, comparison limits, equity and privacy measures, and known confounders. Evaluation shall address food delivered, safety, net public and participant cost, geographic and demographic access, burden on small organizations, service reliability, displacement of existing systems, complaints, and unintended harms. Raw personal data is not required for public release.

## Section 13. Pause and emergency authority

The agency may pause an unsafe activity immediately. Any broader emergency order must identify the trigger, scope, responsible official, evidence, review channel, and an expiry no later than 30 days unless renewed through a public written finding authorized by law. Emergency authority may not suspend whistleblower protection, data restrictions, or access to appeal.

## Section 14. Funding

Final legislation must include a fiscal note and appropriation sufficient for grants, administration, technical assistance, accessible participation, data protection, independent evaluation, and corrective action. Implementation may not rely on uncompensated labor as its core operating model.

## Section 15. Sunset and renewal

Authority expires after the three-year operational pilot plus a defined evaluation closeout period. Renewal or expansion requires a public hearing, independent report, fiscal review, documented response to harms, and affirmative legislative action. Failure to publish required evaluation materials bars expansion but does not erase accrued rights, records duties, or remedies.
