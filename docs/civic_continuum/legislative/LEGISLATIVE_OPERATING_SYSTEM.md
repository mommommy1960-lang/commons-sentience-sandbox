# Legislative Operating System

## Purpose

The Civic Continuum does not treat a declaration of rights as implementation. This operating system carries a bounded public problem through law, budget, administration, measurement, correction, and renewal.

## Non-negotiable design rules

1. **Human dignity:** access rules may not punish poverty, disability, race, family status, immigration status, or inability to navigate bureaucracy.
2. **Reality labeling:** every artifact states whether it is an idea, discussion draft, counsel-reviewed bill, introduced bill, enacted law, funded program, or evaluated program.
3. **Burden follows power:** entities with greater power, scale, and benefit carry greater compliance duties; small organizations receive simplified paths and support.
4. **No self-authorizing power:** rulemaking, delivery, enforcement, audit, and appeal are separated wherever practicable.
5. **Correctability:** people can obtain reasons, challenge errors, receive timely review, and trigger remediation.
6. **Privacy by necessity:** collect the least data required; prohibit sale or unrelated reuse; publish aggregates rather than identities.
7. **Transition engineering:** stabilize essentials immediately, phase obligations by capacity, and test replacements before removing existing supports.
8. **Anti-capture:** disclose conflicts, use open procurement, prohibit exclusive vendor lock-in, protect whistleblowers, and publish performance.
9. **Bounded emergency authority:** every emergency power must name a trigger, decision owner, public log, review path, and automatic expiry.
10. **Evidence before expansion:** renew or scale only when independent evaluation supports it and documented harms are corrected.

## Lifecycle

| Gate | Required output | Stop condition |
|---|---|---|
| 0. Right and harm | Plain-language right, affected people, current harm | Harm is vague or solution is predetermined |
| 1. Jurisdiction | Authority map and existing-law scan | No lawful implementing authority identified |
| 2. Baseline | Evidence register, data limits, fiscal baseline | Core claim cannot be tested |
| 3. Co-design | Public-interest and affected-community record | Directly affected people were excluded |
| 4. Draft | Model language, definitions, duties, remedies | Duties lack funding, owner, or appeal |
| 5. Transition | Immediate interlocks and phased schedule | Existing essential service would be removed first |
| 6. Legislative route | Sponsor, committee, testimony, amendments | Material amendment defeats safeguards |
| 7. Enactment | Final text, appropriation, effective dates | Mandate is unfunded or authority conflicts remain |
| 8. Rulemaking | Public rules, data standard, procurement controls | Closed process or vendor-dependent standard |
| 9. Operation | Public dashboard, complaint channel, audit log | Serious harm cannot be paused or corrected |
| 10. Evaluation | Independent report against preregistered metrics | Results are unavailable or privacy harm exceeds benefit |
| 11. Renewal | Continue, amend, scale, or sunset decision | Automatic permanence without review |

## Control record

Every proposal has a JSON control record validated in continuous integration. The record is not proof of policy merit. It is a refusal to lose essential safeguards while a proposal moves through drafts and amendments.

## Authority architecture

- Legislature: defines rights, duties, funding, boundaries, reporting, and sunset.
- Implementing agency: administers grants and rules within the statute.
- Independent evaluator or auditor: measures outcomes and investigates failures.
- Appeal body: reviews contested decisions independently from first-line enforcement.
- Public and affected communities: testify, inspect aggregate results, submit complaints, and participate in renewal review.

No person or vendor may simultaneously write the operative standard, exclusively supply the required system, judge compliance, and decide appeals.
