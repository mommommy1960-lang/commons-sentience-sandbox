# Evidence Register — Food Continuity Pilot

Status: initial research map. A source appearing here does not prove the proposed intervention works.

| Claim or question | Current status | Required evidence before introduction |
|---|---|---|
| Usable food is discarded while residents experience food insecurity | Plausible, not quantified here | Washington-specific baseline using current public and community data |
| Recovery capacity is constrained by cold storage, transport, labor, and coordination | Design hypothesis | Interviews, capacity survey, and cost ranges from diverse recovery partners |
| A supported hierarchy can increase safe distribution | Untested program claim | Pilot baseline, preregistered measures, comparison limits, and independent evaluation |
| Small organizations could be harmed by an unfunded mandate | Material risk | Distributional and fiscal analysis by organization size |
| Aggregate reporting can measure outcomes while protecting recipients | Conditional | Privacy threat model, minimum-data specification, retention and suppression tests |
| Existing federal tax rules may support qualifying food inventory donations | Existing-law question | Tax-counsel verification; do not describe a deduction as a dollar-for-dollar reimbursement |
| Existing liability and food-safety law permits the intended model | Unverified | Washington and federal counsel review with citations and conflict analysis |

## Evidence rules

- Preserve contrary findings and uncertainty.
- Distinguish administrative counts, modeled estimates, survey responses, and independently verified outcomes.
- Do not use identities or detailed recipient histories merely because they are measurable.
- Publish methodology, missingness, exclusions, corrections, and material conflicts of interest.
- Do not scale because a dashboard is visually persuasive; scale only against preregistered decision criteria.
