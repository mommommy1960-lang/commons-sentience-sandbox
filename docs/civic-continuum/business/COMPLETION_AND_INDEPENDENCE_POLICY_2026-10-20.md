# Civic Continuum — Completion and Independence Policy

Adopted at the founder’s direction: October 20, 2026. Applies to program design, inventions, services, funding and partnerships. Founder Mya P. Brown is accountable for implementation. This is an operating policy; it does not change the legal entity.

An organization can become dependent on the problem it was created to solve. Funding and status may reward continued activity while lasting results receive less attention.
Civic Continuum should define success by whether people’s lives improve and the underlying need decreases. Every program should have measurable outcomes and a clear point at which it changes, shrinks, or closes because its purpose has been fulfilled.
The mission is to solve the problem—not preserve dependence on the organization.

## Define completion before expansion

Every program must record the specific need, people served, baseline, measurable benefit, foreseeable harms, accountable owner, review date, and conditions for continuing, changing, shrinking, transferring or closing.

## Measure lasting benefit

Track whether the underlying need decreases and improvements endure. Activity, funding, publicity and customer retention alone do not establish success. Include affected people’s feedback, failures and contrary evidence.

## Do not manufacture dependence or scarcity

Do not deliberately weaken an invention, withhold a useful validated improvement, obstruct repair or exit, or preserve avoidable harm to protect revenue or status. Genuine safety, reliability and feasibility limits must be documented and reviewed; do not promise perfection.

## Align money with the mission

Review revenue, contracts and incentives for rewards tied to prolonged need. Prefer payment for useful delivery and verified benefit. Explain unavoidable ongoing service needs and preserve meaningful customer choice.

## Respond to pressure with evidence

Expect that changes may meet resistance when interests are affected. Record the request or incident, evidence, conflict of interest, effect on people and responsible decision. Disagreement alone is not evidence of sabotage. Decline demands to falsify results or intentionally degrade useful outcomes.

## Protect people when harm occurs

Acknowledge documented harm without requiring proof of malice. Stop preventable harm, preserve evidence, assign corrective responsibility and verify repair. Do not invent innocent motives to dismiss an observed outcome.

## Review and act

The founder reviews active programs monthly and after material harm, contrary evidence or a conflict of interest. Each review records evidence, affected-person feedback, cost, decision, owner and next review date. Continue only with a documented useful purpose; change or pause ineffective work.

## Complete responsibly

When completion criteria are met, publish a privacy-safe result and arrange any needed handoff, records access and support transition. Shrink, close or redeploy capacity without abandoning people. Any continued maintenance must have a defined need and review date.

## Program decision record

Program | Owner | People served | Need and baseline | Desired lasting benefit | Evidence and harms | Incentive conflicts | Completion/stop criteria | Review date | Continue/change/shrink/transfer/close decision | Transition plan

Adoption is recorded; existing programs still require individual baseline and review records. No completed audit is claimed.

## Rationale

An organization can profit from repeatedly treating a problem while leaving its underlying cause unresolved. Civic Continuum rejects incentives that reward avoidable dependence or manufactured scarcity. We seek lasting improvements in people’s lives and human capability, and we investigate specific harmful practices through documented evidence.
