## What Civic Continuum does

### 1. Accountable AI and systems governance
We design and evaluate ways to make automated and human-assisted workflows more transparent and contestable: explicit permission, bounded authority, revocation, action records, human escalation, failure handling, and correction pathways. We avoid claiming that a policy document alone guarantees safety or compliance.

### 2. Maya Node — permission and action receipts
**Current status:** exploratory discovery and founder-guided synthetic demonstration only. A future *Maya Node Permission & Action Receipt* evaluation may be considered after operational readiness gates are passed and a separate written scope is agreed.

The design goal is simple: before a supported administrative action occurs, verify its explicit authorization; afterward, create a readable record of what was requested, what was authorized, what happened, what failed, and where a person must intervene.

Evidence already documented in the project includes a bounded file-action demonstration, permission checks, blocking after revocation, and saved allow/block receipts. This is not proof of a production-ready hosted customer workflow. The hosted end-to-end path, customer-controlled review/deletion, current full test run, pilot-specific incident contacts, and written business permission remain open readiness gates.

## Exploratory first-partner discussion (not a current paid pilot)

### Why this is the first commercial experiment
A general chatbot is easy to compare with countless others. A narrow workflow that makes authorization and action evidence visible may be more differentiated and more closely aligned with Maya Node's existing demonstrations. This is a hypothesis to test, not established customer demand.

### Best initial partner
One small service business that handles repetitive, low-risk administrative requests through email, forms, spreadsheets, or manual follow-up and is willing to evaluate a single workflow.

**Do not begin with** banking, health, housing eligibility, school discipline, payment movement, refunds, or other consequential automated decisions. Do not use live customer data until every readiness gate has been passed.

### Proposed pilot steps
1. Map one existing workflow and record a baseline.
2. Agree in writing on the exact scope, supported actions, prohibited actions, human review, data handling, and stop conditions.
3. Start with synthetic or redacted data.
4. Test authorization, revocation, duplicate requests, failures, and receipt completeness.
5. Verify customer-controlled record review and deletion, plus the hosted end-to-end path.
6. Compare outcomes and publish only privacy-safe, evidence-supported results, including failures and limitations.

### Proposed measures
- 100% of supported action attempts have a recorded authorization decision.
- All tested attempts after revocation are blocked.
- At least 95% receipt completeness, with every missing receipt investigated.
- Ambiguous, failed, or out-of-scope cases are held for human review.
- Handling time and business usefulness are measured against a baseline, without promising savings in advance.

A pilot is not successful if speed improves but authorization or evidence controls fail.

## Operating principles

- Keep people in control and make meaningful decisions reviewable.
- Measure lasting benefit, not activity or publicity alone.
- Document failures, limitations, and contrary evidence.
- Do not manufacture dependence or intentionally weaken useful solutions to preserve revenue.
