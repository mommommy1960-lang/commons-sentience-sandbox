# Civic Continuum — AI Governance Customer Development Brief
Updated October 13, 2026. Owner: Mya P. Brown. Draft business positioning and outreach copy for review.

## Who we serve
Prospective customers are professionals and teams in school systems and banking organizations who handle personal information and use, procure, supervise, or govern AI-assisted workflows. The focus is accountable AI use, human authority, privacy-related decision boundaries, and evidence of consequential actions—not a claim to provide school physical security or banking services.

Initial interview roles: school/district IT and data leaders, administrators and privacy/procurement staff; bank operations, risk, compliance, information-security and AI-governance teams. These are targeting hypotheses, not established customer relationships.

## Draft pitch
Civic Continuum helps teams examine who can authorize an AI-assisted action, what information the workflow touches, how permission can be withdrawn, and what evidence remains when something goes wrong. We begin with a narrowly scoped, human-delivered review of one redacted workflow and return an authority-and-data map, one bounded adversarial boundary test, and prioritized next actions. Our goal is to make accountability visible and practical before teams expand AI use.

The initial review is educational/design review. It is not legal advice, security certification, penetration testing, regulatory approval, or a production-safety guarantee. No access to live student, customer, account or credential data is needed for initial discovery.

## AI responsibility — Maya Node as the concrete example
Maya Node's documented principle is that technical capability does not confer authority. Its public architecture separates identity, requested operation, authorization scope, expiry, revocation, execution, post-action evidence and human challenge/override.

The customer review therefore maps:
- Who configured the workflow and its rules?
- Who approved deployment and granted permission for this action?
- What action and data access were actually authorized, and for how long?
- Who was responsible for monitoring, intervention and correcting failures?
- What records show the request, permission decision and resulting action?
- How can an affected person obtain an explanation, challenge a decision, withdraw applicable consent or reach human review?

“The AI did it” describes a mechanism; it does not complete the organizational accountability inquiry. Trace the human and organizational decisions without presuming that the programmer alone bears every responsibility. A permission/audit record supports investigation; it does not by itself establish legal liability.

Draft core positioning: **Civic Continuum makes AI responsibility visible: who authorized what, which boundaries applied, what happened, and who must review and correct it.** For school-system and banking customers, pair the technical authority map with a plain-language explanation of relevant notices, rights, responsibilities and escalation paths. Applicable legal rights depend on the actual use case and jurisdiction and require qualified review.
