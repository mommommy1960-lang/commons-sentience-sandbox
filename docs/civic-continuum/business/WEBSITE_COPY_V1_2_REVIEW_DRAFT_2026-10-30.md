## 2. Maya Node product / Workflow Lab page

**Suggested description:** Explore a bounded prototype for reviewing a request, explicit authorization, a permitted file action, and its resulting record.

# Maya Node

## A developing prototype for bounded, human-authorized administrative actions

Maya Node is being developed to make a narrowly defined administrative request easier to review: what was requested, what a person authorized, what happened, what failed, and where human review is needed.

The current demonstration is limited to a bounded text-file workflow. It is not a general-purpose chatbot, autonomous assistant, live email-reply service, or integration with a customer's business systems.

### What the current demonstration shows

1. Prepare an exact text-file request.
2. Review the proposed action and its scope.
3. Explicitly authorize the bounded action.
4. Produce the permitted file once within the defined authorization window.
5. Inspect the resulting action record and any refusal or failure.

### What it does not currently do

- Send or reply to live email.
- Browse websites or operate external customer accounts autonomously.
- Connect to a customer's production database or business system.
- Guarantee permanent retention, immutable records, exact-once behavior across external systems, or complete deletion from every backup.
- Make consequential decisions for people.

### Availability

Maya Node is available only for exploratory conversations and founder-guided demonstrations using invented information. No payment, live customer data, production access, external automation, or paid pilot is currently offered.

A possible future evaluation price has been discussed internally but is not an approved offer. There is no checkout or payment request.

**Call to action:** Request an exploratory conversation about a narrowly scoped workflow review. This is not an order form or commitment to a paid pilot.

## 3. Maya Node evidence and limitations page

**Suggested title:** Maya Node — Evidence, Demonstrations, and Limitations  
**Suggested description:** A dated account of what has been tested in Maya Node, the environment and scope of each result, and the work that remains open.

# Evidence and limitations

Maya Node is an evolving research and software prototype. Evidence is reported with its date, version, environment, test scope, and limitations. Results from one version or test environment must not be presented as proof of a later release or a live customer service.

### Evidence categories

| Area | What may be stated | Important limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Automated tests | A dated test run passed its recorded checks. | The October 28 result of 87 tests belongs to the v18 engineering run only; it is not a fresh v20 result. |
| Hosted founder-account observations | The dated October 29 observations included account creation/read, a revocation-block observation, and deletion of two synthetic fixtures. | These are limited observations in a founder-controlled environment, not independent customer acceptance or proof of universal deletion. |
| Permission and action records | The prototype demonstrates bounded authorization decisions and readable receipts in its supported file workflow. | A receipt records what the system reports; it does not independently prove the truth of every underlying statement. |
| Browser-only examples | Fictional scenarios illustrate policy decisions using invented records. | They do not connect to real organizations, databases, AI models, or alert services. |

### Current limitations

- No live email dispatch.
- No autonomous browsing or external account operation.
- No verified customer production integration.
- No demonstrated permanent or immutable retention.
- Backup recovery and complete customer-controlled deletion remain subject to further verification.
- No independent security certification, regulatory-compliance claim, or zero-error guarantee.
- No live customer pilot or measured business outcome is established.

### What remains before a customer pilot

A narrowly scoped pilot would require a verified hosted end-to-end path, a documented data lifecycle, tested customer review and deletion, operational support and incident ownership, written business permission, and a synthetic-data evaluation before any approved live-data use. No pilot should begin until the specific readiness gates are passed and a separate written scope is agreed.
