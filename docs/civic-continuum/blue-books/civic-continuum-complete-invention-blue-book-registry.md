# Civic Continuum Complete Invention Blue Book Registry

## Purpose

This registry assigns every documented concept its own Blue Book work package. The
goal is mathematical coherence, scientific boundaries, reviewer clarity, and a
credible route from paper to prototype to market.

Paper feasibility is not physical proof. A paper package can show defined mechanisms,
dimensionally consistent equations, visible assumptions, risks, and decisive tests.
Physical claims require calibrated measurement and independent review.

## Universal Blue Book structure

1. Problem, user, and intended benefit
2. Exact function and non-claims
3. System boundary and operating envelope
4. Architecture and component map
5. Mathematical model and dimensional checks
6. Scientific basis and prior art
7. Assumptions, unknowns, and alternatives
8. Materials, software, energy, and cost model
9. Safety, privacy, misuse, and regulatory analysis
10. Failure modes and effects analysis
11. Verification matrix
12. Test method, controls, and instrumentation
13. Pass, fail, and stop criteria
14. Independent reviewer requirements
15. Customer, supplier, pricing, and market path
16. Revision history and evidence ledger

## Drawing pad to market floor

1. Freeze the problem and intended user.
2. Search prior art, standards, existing products, and public disclosures.
3. Create a non-confidential one-page brief.
4. Prepare the private invention disclosure and Blue Book.
5. Decide patent, design protection, copyright, trademark, trade secret, or defensive
   publication with qualified counsel.
6. Obtain independent technical and safety review.
7. Build the smallest safe prototype or software demonstrator.
8. Test against prewritten pass, fail, and stop criteria.
9. Run a supervised customer or community pilot.
10. Establish price, payment, support, warranty, refund, privacy, and recall processes.
11. Qualify suppliers and document quality control.
12. Publish accurate claims, evidence, limitations, and version history.
13. Launch through direct sales, institutional licensing, marketplaces, or qualified
    manufacturing partners.
14. Track complaints, defects, corrections, returns, and safety events after launch.

## Reviewer funding package

Every proposal should end with a two-page investment case containing the problem,
affected user, existing alternatives, proposed advantage, demonstrated evidence,
unknowns, next decisive experiment, experiment-only budget, qualified reviewer,
commercial or public-benefit path, and a stop condition if the result is negative.

That is what makes a proposal fundable on paper: a bounded claim, a visible test, a
credible budget, a qualified reviewer, and a useful outcome even if the boldest
hypothesis fails.
