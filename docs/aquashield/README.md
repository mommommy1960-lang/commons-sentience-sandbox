# AquaShield: public engineering baseline

**Owner:** Mya P. Brown / Civic Continuum  
**Status:** concept and calculation package; not flight-qualified hardware  
**Public version:** 0.1 (2026-09-21)

AquaShield is a proposed **closed-water, dual-use spacecraft subsystem**. It
combines distributed water-wall cells with a sealed, drainable crew chamber for
resistance exercise, recovery, and short-duration refuge. The public design does
not propose an open pool in microgravity.

## What the system is intended to do

1. Store water in modular cells around occupied volume.
2. Use that water mass as one element of a radiation-shielding strategy.
3. Transfer an operationally permitted fraction into a sealed exercise chamber.
4. Generate measurable hydrodynamic resistance with a controlled current and
   load-bearing restraints.
5. Return the water to balanced storage while filtering gas, particles, and
   biological contamination.
6. Isolate any leak without losing the complete water inventory.

## Reality boundary

- Water can contribute useful shielding, but dose reduction depends on particle
  spectrum, geometry, thickness, secondary particles, and mission profile.
- Water resistance can load muscles, but it does not recreate gravity or, by
  itself, guarantee prevention of bone loss.
- A crewed flight unit cannot be claimed from calculations alone. It requires
  radiation-transport analysis, human-factors review, terrestrial testing,
  reduced-gravity testing, and independent safety review.
- The present contribution is an integrated architecture and falsifiable test
  program. Priority, novelty, patentability, and market freedom have not been
  established.

## Next decision gate

The first build is an uncrewed terrestrial coupon-and-loop demonstrator, not a
spacecraft room. It must demonstrate controlled flow, gas separation, leak
isolation, emergency drain, sanitation, force measurement, and mass-balance
closure before any crew test is considered.
