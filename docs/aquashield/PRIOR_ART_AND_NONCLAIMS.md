# AquaShield sources, prior art, and non-claims

## Primary sources informing the baseline

1. NASA, [Water Walls: Highly Reliable and Massively Redundant Life Support Architecture](https://www.nasa.gov/general/water-walls-highly-reliable-and-massively-redundant-life-support-architecture/) — modular water/waste cells, life support, and shielding as integrated habitat functions.
2. NASA, [Passive Radiation Shielding](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/space-tech-research-grants/passive-radiation-shielding-integrating-multilayer-and-multipurpose-materials-into-space-habitat-design/) — multifunction habitat materials and the mass problem.
3. NASA, [Counteracting Bone and Muscle Loss in Microgravity](https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/counteracting-bone-and-muscle-loss-in-microgravity/) — microgravity health risks and countermeasure research.
4. NASA Technical Reports Server, [Biomechanical Modeling of the Deadlift Exercise](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160007731) — resistive loading as a spaceflight countermeasure.

These sources establish that water shielding, integrated water walls, and
exercise countermeasures are active prior fields. AquaShield must not be
presented as inventing those ingredients.

## Candidate contribution to test

The candidate contribution is the controlled combination of segmented water
storage, a reconfigurable fully contained exercise/recovery cell, independently
measured hydrodynamic loading, dry breathing, rapid mechanical egress, and
fault-isolated return to shielding storage. A professional prior-art search is
required before any novelty claim.

## Explicit non-claims

- No claim of preventing cancer, bone loss, muscle loss, or radiation injury.
- No claim of flight readiness or NASA approval.
- No claim that water alone solves galactic cosmic radiation exposure.
- No claim that immersion simulates gravity.
- No claim of patentability, commercial freedom to operate, or priority.
