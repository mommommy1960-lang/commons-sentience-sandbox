# Twenty CARE design improvements

1. Preserve live source audio/media beside every summary.
2. Attach each extracted fact to its exact evidence and timestamp.
3. Keep multiple incident hypotheses until evidence resolves them.
4. Fuse network, device, Wi-Fi, cell, map, and caller location with confidence.
5. Warn on stale, conflicting, impossible, or spoofed location data.
6. Rank only appropriate, available, authorized units.
7. Use travel time, closures, hazards, and capability—not straight-line distance.
8. Display several resource choices and explain the ranking.
9. Transcribe and translate simultaneously while preserving the original language.
10. Train and test for crying, shouting, whispering, disability, accents, and noise.
11. Support silent, open-line, hang-up, text, image, and video calls.
12. Present only agency-approved pre-arrival instructions.
13. Provide one-action human takeover at every stage.
14. Make confirm, correct, and authorize possible without retyping the whole call.
15. Replay the exact evidence behind any high-risk recommendation.
16. Show contradictions and uncertainty as visible warnings.
17. Link duplicate calls while preserving distinct callers and evidence.
18. Flag responder hazards from evidence without demographic profiling.
19. Defend against spoofing, replay, malicious media instructions, and model manipulation.
20. Preserve a tamper-evident audit trail for review, correction, appeal, and learning.

## Pilot success gates

CARE advances only if a controlled, non-live benchmark shows:

- lower time to a correct incident card;
- no unacceptable increase in missed or false dispatch;
- equal or better performance across tested populations and acoustic conditions;
- safe human takeover and failover;
- correct resource eligibility and escalation;
- complete evidence traceability;
- favorable operator workload and usability results.

No live autonomous dispatch is authorized by this document.
