# Confetti Index: Outcome Telemetry Specification

Principal author and framework owner: Mya P. Brown
Program: The Commons Initiative / Reality Audit
Status: Design specification, not a validated social-science instrument
Date: 2026-09-13

## Purpose

The Confetti Index operationalizes a distinction in Mya P. Brown's institutional-design work: system activity is not the same thing as improvement in the human condition the system exists to serve.

It is a falsification-first research framework, not a universal score.

## Two telemetry layers

### System Activity Telemetry
Record what an institution did: contacts, referrals, inspections, assessments, cases opened or closed, money spent, staff hours, interventions, audits, tests, publications, or similar operational measures.

### Human State-Change Telemetry
Record whether the target condition actually changed. Domain-specific measures can include durable stability, exposure to harm, preventable burden, educational access, autonomy, administrative burden, crisis recurrence, and avoidable dependency.

Each domain must define variables, direction of improvement, time horizon, uncertainty, and data provenance before evaluation.

## Core diagnostic

A high-activity and low-improvement pattern is a confetti signal: a reason to investigate whether visible activity is occurring without commensurate verified progress.

The signal is not causal proof. Confounding, case severity, delayed effects, external shocks, selection effects, measurement error, distributional differences, and missing counterfactuals must be tested before stronger conclusions.

## Required evaluation record

Preserve the institutional objective, population and subgroup definitions, activity variables, state-change variables, baseline, comparison period, uncertainty, provenance, alternative explanations, distributional results, preregistered thresholds where applicable, negative results, reviewer notes, and replication status.

## Anti-Goodhart controls

Never reward raw activity counts as success. Preserve raw components behind summaries. Version metric definitions and denominators. Retain subgroup and tail-risk results. Record uncertainty. Audit for cherry-picking, denominator changes, case exclusion, displacement, and delayed effects. Keep qualitative evidence when numerical compression would erase material conditions.

## Optional summary ratio

A domain may define an exploratory activity-to-improvement ratio only when its components are scientifically defensible and interpretable. The ratio must never substitute for underlying measures. A high ratio flags a question for investigation and does not itself prove causation or wrongdoing.

## Reality Audit integration

The framework inherits Reality Audit's discipline: reality first, anomaly second; preserve failure reasons; strengthen tests before claims; distinguish exploratory from confirmatory work; fail closed when evidence is insufficient; maintain provenance and reproducible handoffs.

## Initial validation plan

1. Select one bounded domain with accessible non-sensitive data.
2. Define activity and outcome telemetry before looking for a dramatic result.
3. Construct synthetic high/high, high/low, low/high, and low/low fixtures.
4. Test missingness, delayed outcomes, subgroup reversals, outliers, denominator manipulation, and measurement noise.
5. Demonstrate an INDETERMINATE result when evidence is insufficient.
6. Obtain independent review of definitions and causal language.
7. Only then evaluate a real institutional dataset.

## Claim boundary

This document defines a research and accountability framework. It does not establish a conclusion about any particular institution. Such conclusions require evidence specific to the institution and question being tested.
