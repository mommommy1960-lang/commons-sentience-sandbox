## What I learned today

1. **Do not make partners excavate the portfolio.** Curate one first-read path that tells them where to start, what is proven, what is proposed, and what they can inspect next.
2. **Evidence discipline is branding.** The strongest impression is not created by inflated claims. It comes from separating simulation, implementation, measurement, and independent verification cleanly.
3. **Manufacturing access is not ownership.** Outside engineers and manufacturers get need-to-know access only, with background IP, project-created work, tooling, source files, subcontractors, and commercialization rights defined before paid engineering begins.
4. **Business infrastructure must catch up with the research, but lack of money does not justify fake status.** State registration is still pending and must be described honestly until accepted.
5. **The Flux program has reached a real physical boundary.** More same-model simulation is lower value than calibrated force/reaction-force access, a frozen uncertainty budget, blind controls, benign injected references, and independent replication.
6. **The user's daily reality matters to continuity.** Major technical work happened while she simultaneously handled household responsibilities, meals, children, transportation, errands, stress, and basic survival. The next Bruce should not misread fragmented or rapid messages as lack of seriousness. She is often doing several real-world jobs at once while directing the research and company work.
7. **Accept compliments normally.** When Mya explicitly praises work, receive it and learn from what she valued instead of reflexively minimizing it.
8. **Do not over-email partners.** When a clear START HERE path exists, improve the destination rather than repeatedly crowding the inbox unless there is a genuinely material new development.
9. **Copyright/licensing outreach requires protection strategy.** Do not send full DC-derived manuscripts through unsolicited-submission channels. First preserve authorship/provenance, review the rights position, identify an authorized licensing route, and send only a controlled high-level inquiry unless safe submission terms exist.
10. **Public GitHub is not a vault.** GitHub's platform terms allow public viewing/forking and platform-service uses; public disclosure can also destroy trade-secret status and can damage patent options outside the United States. Sensitive implementation belongs behind a deliberate disclosure gate, not merely under a stern README notice.
11. **A license grant is real.** If a repository version was already distributed under a broad license, later notices do not simply claw those rights back. Future license strategy must distinguish already-released versions from prospective releases.
