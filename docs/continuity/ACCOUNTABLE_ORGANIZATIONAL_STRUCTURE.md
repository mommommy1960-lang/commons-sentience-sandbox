# The Commons Initiative — Accountable Organizational Structure

**Status:** Pre-formation governance framework  
**Founder / accountable authority:** Mya P. Brown  
**Jurisdiction intended:** Washington State, USA  
**Legal entity registration:** PENDING STATE FORMATION  
**Version:** 0.1 — 2026-09-15

This document describes the governance structure intended to govern The Commons Initiative during formation and after registration. It does not claim that a legal entity exists before state formation is completed.

## 1. Purpose

The Commons Initiative develops and studies human-centered systems, AI governance and companion architectures, research-assurance methods, experimental engineering concepts, educational/publication work, and selected physical products. Its governing principle is that increasing technical capability must not silently expand authority over people.

## 2. Accountable authority

Until a different structure is lawfully adopted in executed organizational documents, Mya P. Brown is the founder and final accountable human authority for organizational commitments. No AI system may independently enlarge its own organizational, financial, contractual, publication, licensing, or intellectual-property authority.

## 3. Program structure

The portfolio currently includes distinct workstreams with separate evidence and commercialization gates:

- **MAYA Node:** consent, provenance, bounded authority, revocation, audit and governance controls.
- **SAGE Situated Companion:** situated/persistent companion research with governed continuity and memory.
- **Aurora / Flux research:** simulation, instrumentation and falsification-first advanced-propulsion research; no unverified propulsion claim is converted into demonstrated capability.
- **Nightwatch / Reality Audit:** adversarial assurance, failure preservation and research-integrity controls.
- **Crowned Coil:** physical-product development subject to manufacturing, IP, safety and commercial validation.
- **Publishing / Commons scholarship:** books, papers, manuals and educational work subject to evidence, privacy and third-party-rights review.

A workstream name does not create a separate legal entity or grant authority to a collaborator.

## 4. Decision rights

### Reserved to accountable human authority

Unless expressly delegated in writing:

- contracts and binding organizational commitments;
- expenditure, borrowing, banking and payment authority;
- transfer, assignment or licensing of intellectual property;
- employment and contractor engagements;
- public claims of partnership, endorsement or validated technical capability;
- release of confidential technical materials;
- publication of high-consequence claims;
- changes to core consent, revocation or human-control guarantees.

### Delegation

Delegation must identify scope, duration, permitted actions and revocation conditions. Familiarity, repeated collaboration, memory, technical access, funding, advice or possession of materials does not create implied authority.

## 5. Intellectual property and collaboration boundary

Pre-existing concepts, source material, code, designs, manuscripts, specifications, branding, research records and other intellectual property remain with their lawful owner unless an executed written instrument expressly transfers or licenses defined rights.

No collaborator, adviser, manufacturer, reviewer, contractor, researcher, funder, referral relationship or other third party receives ownership, governance authority, licensing rights or permission over Commons intellectual property merely through access, review, discussion, contribution, referral, funding interest or collaboration.

Third-party contributions must be documented with provenance and applicable rights before incorporation into distributable products.

## 6. Research integrity and evidence status

The organization distinguishes at minimum among:

- proposal / hypothesis;
- simulation result;
- instrument or software validation;
- experimental observation;
- replicated result;
- independently verified result;
- commercial/production capability.

A lower evidence state must not be described as a higher one. Failed tests and falsified hypotheses are retained when material to the audit trail. The operating rule is: **Reality first. Anomaly second. Follow through.**

## 7. AI governance and meaningful human control

AI systems may assist with analysis, drafting, coding, monitoring and other authorized tasks, but learned preference, memory, familiarity, persistence or accumulated trust does not expand permission scope.

Consequential external actions require applicable authorization. Revocation must remain effective across restart and recovery. Unverifiable identity, provenance, authorization or integrity causes privileged operation to fail closed.

## 8. Security and incident handling

Material security failures are preserved, triaged, repaired and regression-tested. Passing a defined test suite is not represented as proof of universal security. Known limitations remain visible until resolved or accepted by accountable authority.

Material incidents should record: time, affected system, evidence, impact, containment, remediation, validation and remaining risk.

## 9. Financial and contracting controls

No person, AI agent or collaborator may commit organizational funds or enter a binding contract without explicit delegated authority. Quotations, discussions, samples and technical evaluations do not themselves authorize paid work.

Material financial commitments should be documented with counterparty, purpose, amount/limit, approving authority and applicable IP/confidentiality terms.

## 10. Conflicts of interest

Material financial, professional or personal interests that could reasonably affect research, procurement, evaluation or organizational decisions should be disclosed to the accountable decision-maker and, where relevant, in public research disclosures.

## 11. Publication, correction and retraction

Public work should identify evidence status and material limitations. Errors are corrected when discovered. Materially invalidated claims are not silently resurrected. Third-party rights, privacy and confidentiality are reviewed before commercial publication.

## 12. Records, provenance and audit

Material decisions, releases, tests, failures, corrections and authority changes should leave durable records sufficient to reconstruct what was known, who or what acted, under what authority, and what changed.

## 13. External representations

Correspondence, exploratory discussions, review requests or technical exchanges must not be described as partnerships, endorsements, referrals, investments or validations unless the external party has explicitly established that relationship.

## 14. Public verification

After formation, the organization should maintain an independently accessible verification page linking, as applicable, to:

- official legal name and jurisdiction;
- state formation/registry record and UBI;
- official domain and organizational contacts;
- accountable governance documentation;
- public technical repositories;
- research publications and persistent identifiers;
- applicable licensing/IP statements;
- correction/security contact path.

## 15. Amendment and version control

Material amendments require accountable human approval and a dated version history. A superseded policy remains available when necessary to interpret historical decisions or permissions.
