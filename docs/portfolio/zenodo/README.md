A DOI preserves provenance. It does not by itself establish scientific validation, safety, patent rights, regulatory approval, manufacturing readiness, or market demand.

## Development lane

Every record moves through the same controlled sequence:

1. Preserve exact source metadata and version relationships.
2. Tighten the user problem, intended claim, non-claims, hazards, metrics, and stop criteria.
3. Complete paper feasibility, prior-art boundaries, architecture, and cost ranges.
4. Run a reproducible simulation, software test, editorial proof, or bounded bench protocol.
5. Obtain independent technical, safety, legal, rights, privacy, accessibility, ethics, or regulatory review as applicable.
6. Build the smallest safe functional prototype with traceable specifications and QA.
7. Pilot with written scope, consent, customer evidence, correction, and support.
8. Qualify production, supplier, packaging, certification, fulfillment, warranty, and change-control requirements.
9. Launch with truthful evidence labels, pricing, support, and release notes.
10. Maintain post-market defect, complaint, correction, pause, and recall controls.

## Safety and publication boundaries

- Keep patent-sensitive, supplier-confidential, security-sensitive, personal, medical, and biological implementation details outside the public repository.
- Treat BRL as a regulated research hold until qualified biomedical, ethics, and regulatory partners approve the next step.
- Treat SAA as bounded satellite and avionics radiation monitoring and mitigation; do not claim magnetic-field repair.
- Treat speculative propulsion, consciousness, phasing, and inertia concepts as falsification-first research, not demonstrated physical capability.
- Treat CRTS as conventional digital manufacturing governance; do not claim matter replication.
